### Sparse-day horizontal compaction (M20, task-230, 2026-03-14)
**Changed from:** Temporal compaction - penalized lessons late in the year
**Changed to:** Sparse-day penalty - penalizes days with 1-3 hours of lessons

**Rationale:** The school's actual requirement is that days with lessons should be "full" (at least 4 hours). A student with Monday 6h, Tuesday 1h, Wednesday 5h would have the same penalty under temporal M20, but Tuesday is unacceptable.

**Implementation:** Groups by `(StudentGroup, Date)`, sums hours, penalizes days where `totalHours > 0 && totalHours < 4`. Weight is 1000 medium (heavy, logical hard constraint).

**Why medium not hard:** Performance - sparse-day enforcement is a "logical hard" constraint for the school, but implemented as medium to avoid making the search space too rigid.

### Afternoon slots reserved for stage (M28, ex H21, task-372/379, 2026-07-18)
referente (17/07, Q17): afternoon slots (14:30-16:30) "vanno usati solo per lo STAGE" — this year's golden grid has 575 real (non-stage) afternoon lessons across 9 groups, a thing not to repeat. H21 penalizes any non-pinned, non-pseudo-teacher Lesson placed in slotIndex >= `TimeslotSequence.AFTERNOON_START_SLOT_INDEX` (6).

**Why hard + pinned-exempt, not a flat hard:** a flat hard would declare the imported golden solution infeasible (575 matches). Since every golden/historical lesson imports as `pinned = true`, excluding pinned lessons (same pattern as `M20`/`horizontallyCompact`) makes H21 hard for the solver's own future placements without touching the historical record.

**Calendar-vs-constraint separation:** `GroupCalendarRule`/H20 (TASK-342) intentionally still mark slots 6-7 OPEN for those 9 groups — that openness is stage capacity, not an invitation for ordinary lessons. H20 answers "is this slot open at all"; H21 answers the narrower "may a non-stage lesson use this open afternoon slot". Pseudo-teacher lessons (the synthetic "Stage" course, TASK-346) are exempt too — those ARE the stage hours the afternoon is reserved for.

### Weekly choice-day constraints (M24/M25, TASK-368, 2026-07-18)
The school's 2026-07-17 docx (`claudedocs/spalmone-2026-27/vincoli-in-parole.md`) revises T11's and T05's availability with CHOICE-DAY semantics a static `RecurringAvailabilityRule` (fixed day-of-week template) can't express: T11 may start at 08:00 on one solver-chosen day/week; T05 must have one fully-free day among his working week, solver-chosen. Both medium tier (scheduling niceties, not safety rules). **Golden measurement (not zero-tolerance):** the golden violates both — M24 -16 medium/15 matches (T11's golden actually has 52 slot-0 lessons, mostly recurring Tuesdays, **contradicting** the task brief that spawned this work, which claimed "zero slot-0 days"); M25 -27 medium/27 matches. ### M21 recalibration (task-365, 2026-07-18)
M21 ("max 1h per subject per day") started as `noConsecutiveSameSubject()` — soft, weight 300, penalizing only ADJACENT same-day same-subject pairs. TASK-243 (2026-03-15) renamed it to M21, promoted soft→medium, and replaced the adjacency join with a `groupBy`+`sum` that penalizes ANY subject with more than 1h on a (group, day) — a semantic broadening that rode along with a Collection/Collector anti-pattern fix, not a deliberate school-backed change. The golden audit (task-347) found the broadened rule fired 861 times for -251,700 medium against the school's own real timetable — the single largest score component overall — because the school routinely teaches multi-hour blocks (analysis.md 4.5: 680 2h + 319 3h + 83 4h + 155 5h + 62 6h blocks, not all labs).

**Recalibration (Q18, referente, 2026-07-17):** "i LABORATORI, Informatica e Disegno possono avere due ore vicine, gli altri POSSIBILMENTE no". Three exemptions now apply before the count: `Lesson.isLab()` (unchanged), a `M21_EXEMPT_SUBJECTS` whitelist (Competenze Digitali, Disegno, Disegno Edile ed Impiantistico — Informatica itself is already lab-tagged via `DataGenerator.getRoomTags()`), and pseudo-teacher lessons (`Teacher.pseudo`, e.g. the synthetic "Stage" course — one long accounting block split across daily timeslots, not a genuine subject repeat; alone 253/861 golden matches, 1,740/2,517 excess hours). The weight also drops from the TASK-243 value of 100/excess-hour to `ONE_MEDIUM` (1/excess-hour), matching the per-unit weight already used by M24/M25 (task-368) rather than M20's deliberately "logical hard" 1000.

**Golden measurement (`GoldenSolutionRegressionTest`):** 867 matches/-252,300 medium before → 452 matches/-621 medium after — a 400x reduction that leaves M21 comparable to (if still larger than) M24 (-16) and M25 (-27), instead of dwarfing every other medium constraint combined as it did before. Residual composition is dominated by ordinary non-lab subjects genuinely repeated same-day-non-adjacent (Scienze 85/86 matches/excess-hours, Matematica 63/64, Lingua Italiana 49/50) — exactly the "possibilmente no" case the school's answer describes. Two subjects stand out as **seed-data flags, not silently whitelisted**: "Ra lab aperto" (29 matches/110 excess hours — `DataGenerator.getRoomTags()`'s own javadoc already flags this subject's name as "suggests a lab activity but no confirmed room") and "Recuperi ed Approfondimenti" (39/82 — real-teacher RA micro-courses, task-366) — neither is named in the school's Q18 whitelist, so both stay penalized; if the school confirms "Ra lab aperto" is in fact lab work, the fix belongs in `DataGenerator.isLabSubject()`/`getRoomTags()` (with explicit permission per `.claude/rules/datagenerator-protection.md`), not in M21's whitelist.

### H15/H16 recalibration (task-349, 2026-07-18)
The golden audit (task-347, `claudedocs/spalmone-2026-27/golden-audit.md`) found H15 ("same-day same-subject must be consecutive") fires 98 times against the school's own historical 2026/27 timetable (−9,420 hard) — a broad distribution across all 13 groups and many subjects (Scienze 24, Lingua Italiana 19, Matematica 11, …), consistent with genuine organic non-adjacency, not a single systemic import artifact. As a flat HARD constraint, this makes the golden solution itself infeasible by the solver's own rules — the school demonstrably tolerates rare splits in practice. H16a/H16b ("consecutive same-subject must share teacher/room") fire **0** times on the same golden data (confirmed both by the original audit and re-measured live by this task's own `GoldenSolutionRegressionTest` run) — there is no organic evidence the school ever splits a teacher or room mid-block, so no recalibration is warranted there.

**Decision (H15): pinned-exempt hard, not a demotion to medium.** Two options were on the table: (a) demote H15 to medium with a calibrated weight (the M21/task-365 playbook), or (b) keep it hard but exempt the golden's own historical splits, mirroring H21/M20's pinned-exemption pattern. (b) was chosen. The Q18 school answer ("gli altri POSSIBILMENTE no" — see `vincoli-in-parole.md`) treats non-adjacency as *tolerated*, not *desired*; a flat medium weight would let the solver freely scatter same-day repeats whenever a small optimization gain outweighs the medium penalty, which is stronger than what the school's own answer implies. H21 already established the precedent for this exact situation (a hard rule the golden's own historical data violates): exempt the historical record via `pinned`, keep the rule hard for everything the solver actually controls. The exemption only ever applies pinned-to-pinned: a hand-pinned lesson that splits against an *unpinned* (solver-controlled) lesson is still penalized, so the solver can't quietly widen a gap next to a human's pin.

**Mechanism** (why it's break-level, not the per-entity `!lesson.pinned` filter M20/H21 use): see `sameDaySameSubjectMustBeConsecutive`'s own javadoc in `SchoolScheduleConstraintProvider.java` — not repeated here to avoid the two drifting apart.

**Golden measurement (`GoldenSolutionRegressionTest`):** H15 before/after: −9,420 hard / 98 matches → **0 hard / 0 matches** (moved from the "calibrated residuals" section of the test to the zero-tolerance section, alongside M20/H21). H16a/H16b: 0 hard / 0 matches, unchanged — newly added as explicit zero-tolerance assertions (previously untested by this guard). `ConstraintVerifier` tests prove the mechanism directly: an all-unpinned split and a one-side-pinned split both still penalize 60 minutes, a both-sides-pinned split penalizes 0, and the pre-existing lunch-crossing test still penalizes 0 (never a split in the first place, task-341, unaffected by this change).

### H15->M15 demotion (TASK-392, 2026-07-22) — SUPERSEDES the task-349 "keep it hard" decision above

The task-349 decision above chose (b) "keep hard + pinned-exempt" over (a) "demote to medium", fearing a medium weight would let the solver "freely scatter same-day repeats". TASK-391's honest R1 benchmark supplied the missing evidence to reverse that: the ONLY hard residual that H15 leaves under the adopted config (ruin 5-20) is **a single Lingua Italiana 2h+1h same-day split** (verified identical across both R1 runs, attributed from raw `lessons.txt` after filtering the ~60 same-day splits that are lab subjects H15 already exempts) — NOT scatter. And that one split is precisely the kind the golden itself does: the golden splits Lingua Italiana **19 times** (of its 98 total H15 splits). A HARD tier therefore penalizes the solver for doing, once, what the school's own accepted timetable does 19 times — the rule as given is **stricter than the school's own practice**.

Per the primary directive (a model rule is judged on "does it reflect authoritative/golden practice?", never on compute cost): "same-day same-subject *must* be consecutive" is logically a PREFERENCE, not a feasibility rule — the golden proves the school treats it as such. So H15 (hard) → **M15 (medium)**. The user confirmed (2026-07-22): "la regola che ci è stata fornita è più rigida di come la scuola stessa la applica, quindi è ragionevole considerarla logicamente un M".

**Mechanism: tier + calibrated weight.** `ONE_HARD` → `ofMedium(20)` (the break-level pinned-exemption and the per-gap-minute weigher are unchanged; the constraint/method were renamed `H15`/`sameDaySameSubjectMustBeConsecutive` → `M15`/`sameDaySameSubjectShouldBeConsecutive`). The weight is NOT the naive `ONE_MEDIUM`: at ONE_MEDIUM a 1h-gap split costs 60 medium while the mediums the solver trades it against (M20 day-fill, M22 compactness) are 1000/day, so the solver would scatter same-day repeats to fill days - the exact task-349 scatter fear, and it would have surfaced in the very first validation solve. `ofMedium(20)` x gap-minutes puts a typical split at ~1200, in the M20/M22 "logical hard medium" family, so a split is only ever accepted when avoiding it would cost MORE than ~1 M20 day (i.e. a genuine forced/zero-slack split, as the golden's own 98 are). Medium (not hard) still never blocks 0-hard at any weight - the weight only orders medium-tier tradeoffs. The exact multiplier (20) is a benchmark-tuning knob against the M15-vs-M20 tension (too high suppresses full-day fill; too low permits scatter). The `ScheduleService` drill-down emits `M15`/`MEDIUM` (was `H15`/`HARD`), and the golden guard asserts M15 on the MEDIUM tier (mirrors M28), still **0** (the 100%-pinned golden's 98 splits stay pinned-exempt). The `ConstraintVerifier` tests are unchanged in value: `penalizesBy(60)` checks the match weight, tier-agnostic.

**Not the same as the M21 case:** M21 (volume cap, 2h/subject/day) is NOT pinned-exempt because it verifies the golden's block practice; M15 KEEPS the pinned-exemption because penalizing a pinned (uncontrollable) split as medium is only score noise. **NOT touched:** the pre-assignment CSP's own `hasH15Violation`/`findH15SafeSlot` hard veto (`PreAssignmentValidator`/`HybridPhase1aService`) — a separate mechanism that keeps PINS clean during pre-assignment; its conservatism doesn't contradict the solver-tier demotion, and its method names still say "H15" (stale but harmless, a distinct concept). **Distinct residual, NOT closed by this:** H17 (-6/-10 in R1) is a genuine solver compactness failure the golden does NOT share (golden H17 = 0), tracked separately in TASK-390. **Validation (pending, laptop):** a re-solve with M15-medium must confirm hard drops (H15 no longer blocks) AND M15-medium stays small / the solver does not exploit the relaxation to scatter (the task-349 fear, empirically untested under medium) — benchmark arm prepared for the laptop (TASK-392).

### H17 afternoon-contiguity + M18 gate recalibration (task-350, 2026-07-18)
Most of task-350's original scope had already landed elsewhere by the time this task closed: H17's lunch-aware adjacency (`TimeslotSequence.slotIndexOf`, task-341), H11's per-group `maxHoursPerDay` reseed from real data (task-343), and H18/M18's calendar-aware start anchor (`SchoolSchedule.getGroupCalendarMinOpenSlot`, task-342) were all confirmed working by the task-347 golden audit. Two items remained.

**H17 afternoon-contiguity: pinned-exempt hard, same pattern as H15 (task-349).** The golden audit found H17 fired −3 hard / 2 matches even after task-341/342/343 landed, root-caused to exactly the two documented afternoon-contiguity exceptions in `analysis.md` section 3 — both fully-pinned historical days: T01/G10/2026-12-17's 13:00 hole (08:00-13:00 + 14:30-16:30) and G08/2027-04-01's stage+RA 12:00-14:00 double hole. Rather than accept these as a permanent medium-tier carve-out, `verticallyCompact()` now applies the exact H15 break-level pinned exemption: a gap is excused only when BOTH the previous and next lesson are pinned, so the golden's own historical gaps stay legal while the solver can never create a gap of its own (a break touching even one unpinned lesson still fires). See `verticallyCompact`'s own javadoc in `SchoolScheduleConstraintProvider.java` for the mechanism (not repeated here, same anti-drift rationale as H15/H16 above). `ScheduleService.addH17Violations` (the UI drill-down) was consolidated onto the same semantics — canonical slot-index gap counting (`TimeslotSequence.slotIndexOf`) instead of raw hour arithmetic, plus the identical pinned-both-sides check — so the drill-down never disagrees with the constraint score. **Golden measurement:** −3 hard / 2 matches → **0 hard / 0 matches**, moved to the zero-tolerance section of `GoldenSolutionRegressionTest`.

**M18 gate: per-(group, date) calendar-open-slot count, not the group's static field.** The task-342 review flagged that M18's applicability gate still selected lessons via `studentGroup.maxHoursPerDay <= 5`, a single static value per group, even though the start anchor itself had already become calendar-aware. For a mixed-calendar group like G12 (Mon-Wed 5 open slots, Thu 8, Fri 7, `DataGenerator.GROUP_CALENDAR_PATTERNS`), the static field (reseeded to 8 by task-343, since the group's overall daily max is 8) excluded the group from M18 **entirely** — including its genuinely short Mon-Wed days, exactly the days the rule is meant to cover. The gate now calls `SchoolSchedule.getGroupCalendarWindow(group, weekday, date)[0] <= 5` per (group, date) — a combined lookup that resolves the group's calendar range once for both the gate and the calendar-aware anchor below it (a standalone `getGroupCalendarOpenSlotCount`, the same helper `dailyQuota()`/M20 uses, still exists for single-value callers) — instead of the lesson's static `studentGroup.maxHoursPerDay`. This widens the set of evaluated days considerably: every group's short Friday, G12's Mon-Wed, G10's Monday, not just G14 (the only group whose static field was ever ≤5). **Golden measurement:** 0 hard / 0 medium / 0 matches both before and after the gate change — on every newly-gated short day in the golden data, the human schedule already starts at the group's first calendar-open slot, consistent with H17's own finding that human planners build compact, gap-free schedules. Newly pinned in `GoldenSolutionRegressionTest` as zero-tolerance (previously untested by this guard, since the pre-350 gate only ever covered G14).

### Course-timing constraints (task-369, 2026-07-18)
The 2026-07-17 docx (`claudedocs/spalmone-2026-27/vincoli-in-parole.md`) names three course-timing rules not previously in the model: T03's "Sicurezza before stage", T20's subject-ordering preference, and a general "possibilmente >=1h/week per subject" continuity clause. All three are cross-checked independently in Python (`scripts/analyze/derive-golden-availability-checks.py`'s TASK-369 section) against the Java constraint streams, same methodology as M24/M25.

**M26 - Sicurezza before stage.** Every Sicurezza lesson for a group must be dated strictly before that group's `StagePattern.startDate` - a lesson ON the start date itself is not "before the start", so it's penalized too (matches `stageBlackout`'s own inclusive-of-startDate boundary; reachable at all only because Sicurezza is professionalizzante, so H4 doesn't already block it during stage). `sp.startDate` is nullable (`StagePattern.expandDates`'s own null guard), so the constraint null-guards it the same way. Implemented as a single hardcoded subject constant (`BaseConstraintProvider.SICUREZZA_SUBJECT`), not a generic subject-before-stage rule table - the javadoc notes the generalization path (widen to a `Set`/`Map`) if a second such rule is ever needed. Groups without a seeded `StagePattern` (every 2026/27 group except G07, G11, G12) are a structural no-op. **Golden measurement:** 0 hard / 0 matches - G07 is the only StagePattern-seeded group (of the 3 seeded) with any Sicurezza hours at all in the golden data (G11/G12 simply don't teach Sicurezza), and its 12 golden Sicurezza lessons (2026-09-15..2026-12-02) all fall before its 2027-03-23 stage start. T03 already applied this rule by hand.

**M27 - T20 subject start-period preference.** Per-subject window on the week offset (elapsed weeks since `TimeslotSequence.SCHOOL_YEAR_START`, 0-indexed) of a course's FIRST scheduled lesson: Merceologia/Arredi Tessili ("iniziare subito") must start by week 3; Tecnologia tessile/Storia della moda ("devono iniziare più tardi") must not start before week 4; Figurino/Consulenza d'immagine/Sviluppo Sostenibile ("quando si vuole") are absent from the table entirely, so never evaluated. Windows derived from the golden's actual first-lesson dates per (group, subject): the early pair's latest golden start is week 2 (G09/Arredi Tessili, 1-week margin below the week-3 cutoff); the late pair's earliest golden start is week 6 (G01/Storia della moda, 2-week margin above the week-4 floor). **Golden measurement:** 0 hard / 0 matches - every one of the 12 golden T20 group/subject pairs falls inside its window with margin.

**S12 - Weekly subject presence.** NOT a restoration of S9 (disabled, task-273): S9 penalizes MORE than 1 lesson/subject/week (an upper-bound anti-concentration rule that fights M22's calendar compactness); this docx clause ("possibilmente almeno 1 ora/settimana... esclusi periodi di stage") is the opposite direction, a lower bound - a subject shouldn't go a whole active week with zero hours. Rather than detect absent weeks directly (would require enumerating each (group, subject)'s full active-week domain, not expressible as a per-entity/pair constraint stream), S12 rewards each distinct (group, subject, ISO week) with >=1 lesson once, regardless of how many lessons land in it - maximizing this is equivalent to spreading hours across as many weeks as possible. Weight `ONE_SOFT` (light, matching the task brief), pseudo-teacher lessons excluded (mirrors M21's Stage exclusion). A stage week can't earn the reward by construction (H4 keeps non-prof lessons off stage days), so the docx's "esclusi periodi di stage" carve-out falls out for free without an explicit filter. **Golden measurement:** +5,623 soft / 5,623 matches (measured directly against the 100%-pinned golden import, not pinned-exempt like S9/M20 - the point is to verify the school's own continuity practice, same reasoning M24/M25 already apply). The naive Python cross-check first read 5,625: the gap traces to `report.unmatchedNoTimeslot`'s 15 rows (G04/Scienze + G08/RA, dated 2027-06-14/15, past `SCHOOL_YEAR_END`=2027-06-10) which never become pinned Lessons and were each the sole lesson in their (group, subject, week) combo - excluding rows outside the Timeslot horizon in the Python script reproduces 5,623 exactly.

### Professionalizzanti at day edges (H22, task-370, 2026-07-18)
The 2026-07-17 docx (`claudedocs/spalmone-2026-27/vincoli-in-parole.md`) gives G12's precise professionalizzanti rule: "professionalizzanti solo il venerdì, oppure giovedì/mercoledì alla prima o all'ultima ora, oppure alle prime due o alle ultime due ore. ### Weekly stability soft constraint (S13, task-375, 2026-07-18)
TASK-351's recurrence research (`claudedocs/design/weekly-template-redesign-2026-07-17.md`) found NO-GO on a template-first pipeline rewrite (41.5% strict within-segment recurrence, 72% modal ceiling, ~16 distinct weekly grids/group) but recommended capturing the real 41.5-72% recurrence as a cheap soft-constraint *preference* instead: reward a lesson whose (StudentGroup, weekday, slotIndex) cell matches the same cell exactly one week earlier on (teacher, subject).

**Directionality resolves the design doc's bidirectional double-count risk.** The doc's prose ("matches the lesson in the same slot of the adjacent week(s)") reads as symmetric, but a symmetric `abs(dateDiff)==7` self-join would let one recurring (week N, week N+1) pair earn TWO rewards - once as N's "next week matches", once as N+1's "previous week matches" - double-counting a single recurrence event. The task brief's WHAT #1 is explicit that only the PREVIOUS week counts, so the join key is directional by construction: `current.date.minusDays(7)` equals `previousWeek.date`, an equality that can only ever produce the `(current, previousWeek)` ordering, never its reverse. Each Lesson can be "current" (rewarded) for at most one prior-week match; a Lesson that is itself someone else's anchor earns its OWN separate reward against ITS OWN previous week, which is intended (N real weeks of recurrence = N distinct reward events), not a duplicate.

**Pinned semantics are asymmetric by design.** The rewarded ("current") side excludes pinned lessons (`!lesson.pinned`) - mirrors S9's historical pinned exemption, so a pinned-heavy solution can't inflate this score just by being pre-fixed. The anchor ("previousWeek") side has no such filter: a pinned lesson (calendario/golden pin, or a future lab-rotation pin) is at least as authoritative a template cell as a solver-placed one, and an unpinned lesson reproducing it deserves the same reward as reproducing another solver-placed lesson. Consequence: the 100%-pinned golden fixture measures S13=0/0 (zero eligible earners, not zero recurrence) - the golden regression test's own comment documents this pin.

**Weight: 2 soft**, deliberately low per the design doc's "start low" guidance - between S12's presence-only 1 and S1's per-gap-minute 2, well under S7/S8/S10's 10/20/50. This is a nudge toward human-like weekly regularity, not a rule strong enough to fight M20 (daily quota)/M22 (calendar compactness)/S9 (disabled, would directly oppose it if re-enabled). Reward-only by construction, so a closed or absent previous week (closure, stage window, before the course's first lesson) produces no join match and is neutral, never penalized - no explicit `SchoolHoliday`/`StagePattern` lookup needed. Pseudo-teacher (Stage/Esame) lessons are excluded on both sides, mirroring M21/S12's precedent.

**A/B protocol for m-11 (AC2, not yet executed - no live solve was run for this task):** one full solve WITH S13 active vs one full solve WITH S13 disabled (`debugConstraintsDisabled`-style toggle or a temporary comment-out), same seed data, same solver time budget. Compare: (1) stability metric = % of unpinned Lesson-hours whose previous-week cell matches on (teacher, subject) - re-derivable via the same join logic in Python or by reading `S13`'s constraint-match count off `/api/schedule/analysis`, normalized by total unpinned lesson-hours; (2) hard/medium score must not regress between the two runs (S13 is soft-tier and reward-only, so it cannot itself create a hard/medium violation, but it competes for local-search moves against M20/M22/S9-successor constraints and could indirectly worsen them); (3) soft score composition - confirm S13 isn't just cannibalizing S1/S7/S12's reward budget without a net stability gain. Run in the imminent m-11 solve session; do not fabricate these numbers ahead of an actual solve.

### Weekly template construction (L2, TASK-381, 2026-07-20)
Run 12/13 (TASK-381's own notes above) measured the gap this sub-task closes: weekly recurrence among UNPINNED placed lessons stuck at 16.9% under the old pure day-clustered round-robin CH, vs the golden's 41.5-72% - the school's own process is template-first (a recurring weekly pattern per group, broken only where stronger constraints interfere), and run 12/13 both ended on the SAME residual H3 pair (G05, a stacked Scienze/T09 vs Matematica/T08 lesson) root-caused to G05's calendar capacity being EXACTLY 990h against 990h demand - zero slack, so placement is a perfect bipartite matching the old round-robin CH could not discover by chance, but a weekly template produces BY CONSTRUCTION.

**Two-class construction, same phase, no XML/renumbering.** `DayClusteredConstructionPhase` (still solverConfig.xml's single phase 1) now runs template-build-and-stamp FIRST, with the pre-existing round-robin CH demoted to its repair tail (unchanged mechanics - `Moves.change` plumbing, `forcePlaceLeftovers` as the final fallback - both loops already skip any lesson whose `timeslot` is no longer null, so nothing else needed to change). `WeeklyTemplateBuilder` (new, `com.school.planner.solver`) is deliberately pure Java with zero Timefold/CDI coupling - every input (calendar shapes, quota caps, blocked cells, weekly-hour targets) is resolved by the phase from data it already caches and passed in as plain data, so the greedy algorithm itself is unit-testable without a solver context.

**Build order (hardest-constrained first, deterministic).** For each group with unpinned lessons: a `GroupWeeklyShape` is derived from the MODAL (majority, not per-date-exact) `[minSlot,maxSlot]` window per weekday and, for the 3 quota groups (G04/G08/G13), the modal non-zero lab/aula hour budget per weekday - rare calendar exceptions (closures, one-off overrides) don't distort the "typical week" the template is built from, and correctness never depends on this being exact (see below). Teachers are then processed in ascending free-capacity order (fewest open weekly cells first - e.g. T28's Thursday-only pattern), each course requesting `max(1, round(remainingUnpinnedHours / activeWeeks))` hours/week, preferring one contiguous 2h block when the weekly target allows it (matching the golden's 680x2h practice) before falling back to singles, then to the afternoon (M28 last resort) only when the morning genuinely can't fit the week's demand. Placement is always at the group's next contiguous free slot that weekday (compactness/H17 by construction - no gaps a template cell can ever introduce) and respects H7/H11 daily caps and the quota lab/aula per-day budget throughout. ### ~~Student group gap avoidance~~ (S5, REMOVED)
**HISTORICAL NOTE:** This constraint was removed and superseded by M13 (vertically compact).

The original S5 penalized gap-minutes between a student group's daily lessons with a weight of 500, to strongly incentivize contiguous blocks. H8 enforces that student groups have at least 4 lessons on any day they attend, but without strong gap avoidance those lessons could be scattered (e.g., 08:00, 10:00, 12:00, 14:00).

**Alternative considered: hard no-gap constraint (H10).** A hard constraint forbidding any gaps in a student group's daily schedule was benchmarked against the soft approach. Results (6-minute runs on cartesian-shadow-lab-blocks, 8,693 lessons):

| Metric | Hard (H10) | Soft (S5=500) |
|--------|-----------|---------------|
| CH time | 231s | 155s |
| Final hard score | -1,975k | -1,185k |
| Final soft score | -4.3M | -52.1M |

The soft approach won: 40% better hard score and 76s faster CH. **Outcome:** S5 was eventually removed and superseded by M13 (vertically compact), which provides better vertical compaction through a different mechanism.

### Informatica after stage (M5, task-015)
MODA and FA student groups need ~33% of their Informatica hours scheduled AFTER their stage period ends, for thesis writing ("tesina"). This is implemented as M5 (medium constraint), which rewards (+1 MEDIUM) Informatica lessons scheduled after the stage `endDate`.

**Why medium, not hard:** The school indicated "it shouldn't be hard for sure, but it should happen" — meaning it's a preference, not a requirement. Medium constraints guide the solver without blocking valid solutions.

**Target groups:** G01, G05, G09, G02, G06, G10, G12

**Mechanism:** Join each Lesson with its student group's StagePattern, filter for Informatica subject and target groups, reward if lesson date is after stage ends. No penalty for lessons during/before stage (just no reward).

### ~~Lab recurring pattern~~ (M17, task-113, REMOVED)
**HISTORICAL NOTE:** This constraint was removed. Functionality may have been moved to pre-assignment pipeline.

For single-day lab courses (e.g., 6h/week), all blocks should be on the same day of week within each rotation period. This was a critical human planner heuristic — labs recur on the same day.

**Why period-aware:** Rotation courses (e.g., T28's acconciatura) teach different student groups in different trimester periods. Within each period, blocks ARE on the same day (Thursday), but across periods the groups rotate. The constraint must only compare blocks within the same `LabRotation` period.

**Original Implementation:**
- M17: Penalizes blocks with same course but different days (for single-day courses)
- M17.2: Limits distinct days to expected count (for multi-day courses)
- Both use `LabBlock.labRotation` field to group by rotation period
- Blocks without `labRotation` (null) are compared across the entire year

**Why medium, not hard:** Shared resources (rooms, teachers) can make same-day patterns infeasible in some cases. The medium weight guides the solver without blocking valid solutions.
